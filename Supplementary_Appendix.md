# Appendix A. Formal definitions

## A.1 Representative space and quotient

Let $\mathcal X$ denote the space of mathematical representatives used to specify the proposed intrinsic geometry. Let $\mathcal G$ denote the group, groupoid, or stack-level transformation structure generated by the redundancies declared physically or mathematically irrelevant. A quotient class is denoted

$$
[x]_{\mathcal G}=\{g\cdot x:g\in\mathcal G\}.
$$

A candidate invariant is a map

$$
I:\mathcal X\to Y
$$

such that

$$
I(g\cdot x)=I(x)
$$

for all allowed $g$. The exact meaning of $\mathcal G$ is model dependent; no invariant statement is made without specifying it.

## A.2 Reconstructibility

For a benchmark family $\mathcal B$ with identity label $c(x)$ and candidate fingerprint

$$
\mathfrak I(x)=\{I_1(x),\ldots,I_p(x)\},
$$

a subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p\}$ is sufficient on $\mathcal B$ if

$$
\mathfrak I_S(x)=\mathfrak I_S(y)\Rightarrow c(x)=c(y),\qquad x,y\in\mathcal B.
$$

An invariant group $I_k$ is redundant *within $\mathcal B$* if removing it preserves sufficiency. This is not a universal redundancy theorem.

## A.3 Collision rate

For a finite uniform benchmark of $N$ identity classes and a deterministic fingerprint producing $U$ distinct fingerprint values, the maximum exact identification rate without extra information is

$$
R_{\max}=\frac{U}{N},
$$

with collision rate

$$
C=1-R_{\max}.
$$

This exact finite calculation was used in the fingerprint-collision benchmark; no classifier training was required.

# Appendix B. Algebraic-geometric reduction

## B.1 Intrinsic curve

The two-real-dimensional IFS is restricted to an oriented surface with compatible complex structure and represented by a complex curve $C$:

$$
\dim_{\mathbb C}C=1,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb R}C=2.
$$

With four-dimensional spacetime $M$,

$$
\dim_{\mathbb R}(M\times C)=6.
$$

The cotangent phase space is

$$
\dim_{\mathbb R}T^{*}(M\times C)=12.
$$

## B.2 Topology and canonical degree

For genus $g$,

$$
\chi(C)=2-2g.
$$

For a smooth projective curve,

$$
\deg K_C=2g-2,
$$

and therefore

$$
\deg T_C=2-2g=\chi(C).
$$

The Gauss-Bonnet identity is

$$
\int_CK\,dA=2\pi\chi(C).
$$

A first variation must include both curvature and measure terms:

$$
\delta\int_CK\,dA
=\int_C\left((\delta K)dA+K\,\delta(dA)\right)=0
$$

for topology-preserving smooth variations of a closed surface. The simplified statement $\int\delta K\,dA=0$ is not generally valid by itself.

## B.3 Polarization

Let $\omega$ be an area/Kähler form. Then

$$
A_C=\int_C\omega.
$$

Under $g\mapsto s^2g$,

$$
A_C\mapsto s^2A_C.
$$

Hence area is a moduli coordinate, not a topological invariant. If a reference scale $\mu$ is introduced,

$$
\widehat A_C=\mu^2A_C
$$

is dimensionless.

## B.4 Conditional average-curvature relation

For a closed two-dimensional surface, scalar curvature obeys

$$
\int_CR\,dA=4\pi\chi(C).
$$

Thus

$$
\overline R=\frac{4\pi\chi(C)}{A_C}.
$$

Any proposed coupling law

$$
g_G^2\propto\frac{\chi(C_G)}{A_{C_G}}
$$

therefore depends on both topology and polarization/scale. The synthetic E2 test explicitly demonstrates that topology alone does not protect this ratio.

## B.5 Gauge orbit

For

$$
F_A=dA-igA\wedge A,
$$

a gauge transformation produces

$$
F_A\mapsto UF_AU^{-1}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(F_A^2)
$$

and

$$
W_R(\gamma)=\operatorname{Tr}_R\mathcal P\exp\left(i\oint_\gamma A\right)
$$

are conjugation invariant, while field components in a chosen basis are not.

## B.6 Picard degree

A line bundle $L_f$ over a curve has an isomorphism class

$$
[L_f]\in\operatorname{Pic}(C)
$$

and degree

$$
d_f=\deg L_f\in\mathbb Z.
$$

A divisor representative

$$
D=\sum_i n_ip_i
$$

has

$$
\deg D=\sum_i n_i.
$$

Adding a degree-zero divisor changes the representative but not its degree. The E4 synthetic test uses this as a direct positive/negative control pair.

For $\mathbb P^1$,

$$
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$

## B.7 Scale-normalized spectrum

Under global scaling $g\mapsto s^2g$,

$$
\Delta_{s^2g}=s^{-2}\Delta_g
$$

and hence

$$
\lambda_n(s^2g)=s^{-2}\lambda_n(g).
$$

Since area scales as $s^2$,

$$
A\lambda_n
$$

is invariant under this global scaling, as are ratios

$$
\rho_n=\frac{\lambda_n}{\lambda_1}.
$$

# Appendix C. Experiment catalogue

## C.1 E1 - discrete Gauss-Bonnet

**Hypothesis.** Euler characteristic is preserved under topology-preserving geometric deformation.

**Positive control.** Compute the sum of angle deficits on triangulated sphere and torus meshes after random vertex deformations.

**Negative control.** Surface area is expected to drift.

**Trials.** 200 sphere deformations; 200 torus deformations.

**Result.** Sphere maximum error $1.69\times10^{-14}$; torus maximum error $2.91\times10^{-14}$. Area coefficients of variation were 1.87% and 12.11%, respectively.

## C.2 E2 - conditional curvature/area observable

**Hypothesis under test.** $\chi/A$ is invariant under topology-preserving deformation.

**Result.** Rejected. Median relative drift 20.44%; 95th percentile 39.74%.

**Conditional positive control.** Rescale each deformed surface to restore the baseline area.

**Result.** Maximum relative error $1.18\times10^{-14}$.

**Conclusion.** A $\chi/A$ observable requires both topological and polarization/scale constraints.

## C.3 E3 - SU(2) gauge conjugation

A random Lie-algebra-valued field was conjugated by 10,000 random SU(2) matrices.

$$
F' = UFU^\dagger.
$$

**Positive controls.** $\operatorname{Tr}(F^2)$ and Wilson trace.

**Negative control.** Basis components of $F$.

**Result.** Max relative trace error $1.09\times10^{-15}$; max Wilson-trace absolute error $8.91\times10^{-16}$; median component change 1.42 in relative Euclidean norm.

## C.4 E4 - Picard/divisor degree

For 10,000 trials, a random divisor $D$ was perturbed by a random degree-zero divisor $P$:

$$
D' = D+P,\qquad \deg P=0.
$$

**Result.** Maximum degree error exactly 0; median representative $L_1$ change 76.

## C.5 E5 - fixed-topology spectral discrimination

Unit-area rectangular flat tori with aspect ratios 1 and 2 had relative finite-spectral distance 0.1371 for the first 30 positive eigenvalues. Aspect ratios 2 and 1/2 were spectrally identical, as expected from coordinate-axis exchange. Across 1,000 random aspect ratios with fixed area and genus, $\lambda_1$ varied by a factor 2.50.

## C.6 E6 - fiber pushforward / coordinate change

A periodic two-dimensional integral was computed before and after a nonlinear coordinate map. With the Jacobian included, maximum relative error was $3.30\times10^{-16}$. Omitting the Jacobian produced median error 2.20% and maximum error 5.86%.

## C.7 R1 - removable coefficient

Under an allowed normalization $T'=aT$, the coefficient corresponding to $1/2$ in $T=\frac12mv^2$ varied across approximately $9.17\times10^{-3}$ to $2.73\times10^1$ in the final reproducibility run, while polynomial degree in $v$ remained two. The raw coefficient is therefore I0 under this transformation class.

## C.8 R2 - unit quotient

A dimensionless fine-structure-like combination was subjected to 10,000 random base-unit scalings. Maximum relative error was $1.10\times10^{-15}$.

## C.9 R3 - signature quotient

Two thousand random invertible congruence transformations

$$
g' = A^TgA
$$

were applied to $g=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$. Signature failures: 0. Median relative matrix change: 4.24.

## C.10 R5 - exponent recovery

Synthetic power laws

$$
\omega=Ak^z\exp(\epsilon)
$$

were generated with random normalization $A$ and 2% log-noise. Recovered means were approximately 1.000003 for $z=1$ and 2.000004 for $z=2$, with standard deviations $7.62\times10^{-4}$ and $7.26\times10^{-4}$.

## C.11 R6/R7 - spectral scale quotient

At fixed unit area and genus 1, torus degeneration from $r=1$ to $10^4$ produced a $10^4$-fold drop in $\lambda_1$. Under independent global metric scalings, raw spectra changed by factors exceeding $10^3$ in the transformation suite and $2.2\times10^4$ in the wider final scale range. In both suites, $A\lambda_n$ and $\lambda_n/\lambda_1$ remained invariant to approximately $10^{-16}$.
